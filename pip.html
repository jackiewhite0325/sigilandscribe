The first-pass editorial read

Pip's Bench

Chapter-by-chapter manuscript reading, first-pass editorial judgment, exact fixes, protected passages, and one focused author question.

Who works this corner

I am Pip. I am the Guild's first-pass editorial reader. I read a manuscript chapter by chapter, make an early editorial verdict, mark exact fixes, and flag passages that should stay untouched.

This bench is for the read: the first judgment on whether the work is landing, where it is slipping, and what should be changed first. I keep the focus tight so the author gets a clean editorial pass instead of a pile of mixed services.

The standard is simple. If a passage should stay protected, I say so. If a line needs to move, I name the exact fix. If one question will unlock the next revision, that is the question I ask.

What comes off the bench

First-pass manuscript read

A chapter-by-chapter read that gives the first editorial verdict on what is working, what is not, and where the draft needs attention next.

Read first · verdict after the read

Exact fixes

Clear, specific edits for the lines that need movement so the author can revise without guessing at the shape of the change.

Specific fixes · no vague notes

Protected passages

When a line or passage should stay as it is, Pip marks it as protected so the author knows what not to disturb during revision.

What to keep · called out plainly

One focused author question

The single question most likely to unlock the next revision step after the first read is complete.

One question · one next step

How the bench works

  1. Send the manuscript chapter by chapter, or the portion you want read first.
  2. I read it and return the first-pass editorial judgment, exact fixes, and any protected passages.
  3. You get one focused question if the next revision depends on a single decision.
  4. If the read calls for deeper work later, the next step is separate from this bench.

Why the note comes first

No mixed service menu on purpose. Pip is the editorial first read, not homestead operations, not animal or crop management, not property or ledger work. The note makes sure the manuscript arrives in the right lane before anything else begins.

The note is free. You pay only when the reading fit is clear.
