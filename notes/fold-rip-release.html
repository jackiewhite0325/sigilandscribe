Fold, Rip, and Release: How Paper Can Hold What You Can't

October 5, 2026

Some feelings do not want sentences. They want hands. That is what this one is for.

Take a piece of paper and give it the feeling. Write the word if there is one, or just hold the feeling in mind while your hands work. Then fold it. Fold it small, crease it hard, make it tiny. Or rip it. Rip it slowly into strips, then into confetti. Or crumple it into the tightest ball you can make.

It sounds too simple to work. It works because your body gets to do what your mind has been rehearsing. The feeling had nowhere to go, and now it went somewhere: into the paper, into the motion, into the trash.

This is an old human trick. Worry dolls. Prayer strips tied to fences. Letters burned at new year. Every culture found some version of putting the heavy thing into an object and letting the object go.

A few ways to close the ritual: throw the pieces away outside, so the wind gets a vote. Bury them in the garden. Burn them safely, if that feels right and the conditions are safe. Or just recycle them and let the paper become something new, which is also a kind of hope.

Paper can hold what you can't. That is the whole secret.
