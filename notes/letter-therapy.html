Letter Therapy: Writing the Words You Couldn't Say

October 4, 2026

Everyone has a letter they never wrote. To someone who left, someone who hurt them, someone they never got to thank. This post is about writing it.

Letter therapy is simple: you write the letter you could not say out loud, and you do not send it. The point was never the other person. The point is that the words finally exist somewhere outside your chest.

A few ground rules that help:

Write it by hand if you can. Slow is good here.
Say the unsayable. No one will read this unless you choose to share it. Be honest past the point of politeness.
End it deliberately. Sign it, date it, fold it. Give the feeling a finished shape.
Decide what it is for. Some letters get kept. Some get burned. Some get torn up and thrown away, which is its own kind of sending.

Grief, anger, gratitude, apology. They all fit in an envelope. You do not need the other person's participation to finish a conversation with yourself.

If you try this, the Quiet Room has writing spaces that save to your device, and nobody has to know.
