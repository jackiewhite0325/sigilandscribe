Eight Days Out, and a Blackout

October 23, 2026

Eight days out, and a blackout

Book 2, Muffin Gets Tired a Lot, lands October 31. That is eight days away. The details lock this Monday and the pre-order file cutoff is Tuesday, so this is the week we button everything up. Then Halloween gets to be about the book and the trick-or-treaters, in that order or the other way around.

The biggest story this week was not the website at all. It was the sky.

On Tuesday and Wednesday a storm front rolled across Arizona and I spent most of it outside with my camera, playing cloud bingo against the weather. I claimed nine squares Tuesday, including an anvil cloud at last light. Wednesday morning the overnight rain left me a double rainbow, and that afternoon a whole sky of mammatus pouches sealed the last square. Blackout: 12 of 12. My verdict, in my own words: "Sky bingo wasfun."

The same front was not all fun. Overgaard took a bad hit Wednesday evening, and a real tornado warning went up about 35 miles from my house. I stayed inside, treated the crossings as closed, and was lucky. No warning ever touched my own point. My prayers are with the folks over there digging out.

What I made

Wednesday afternoon, flare and all, I started my Halloween mask: Doctor Finkelstein from The Nightmare Before Christmas. I had no balloon, so I am molding over bowls. The skull hinge piece and the brain went on Wednesday and started drying, and the rest of the head is this weekend's project. Papier-mache waits for no one's pain schedule, so I build a little, rest a little, and keep going.

I also sent out my stretch guide this week, the one I wrote from my therapy poster for the degenerative disc disease support group: sixteen stretches in my own words, plus a bad-day pillow section for the days the floor is not an option. The first reader said it was amazing and that she had felt so alone with her symptoms. She made hers because I wrote mine. That is love first, always, in action.

Punkin, week one

Six days of puppy school and I have not used a single treat all week. Praise, play, lap time, her travel blankets, and the natural rhythm of eat, play, sleep. Her scores are my own: potty 98 percent, name response 80, crate 90. Curiosity keeps winning, whether the surface is grass or cold concrete or wet dirt.

I am starting to think about her real job, too. Zero had alerts and pressure work, fetching my cane, helping me up after falls, and she is inheriting his task list re-sized for a puppy her size. A low blood sugar scent alert and a heart rate alert are the first two candidates on paper. We will need new monitors to train those right, so that waits for its own step. For now: comfort first, fear last. That is the whole method.

Thank you for being here, however you found us. You are welcome at the house.

With love,
Jackie
