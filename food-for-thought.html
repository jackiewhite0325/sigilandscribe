Food for Thought

A gentle place to learn what food does for you. Each nutrient below comes with its buddy, the partner nutrient that helps it do its work.

Friendly learning, not medical advice. Your care stays with your own team.

Vitamins

Vitamin A

What it does

Vitamin A helps your eyes adjust to dim light, supports your immune system, and keeps skin healthy.

Where to find it

Carrots, sweet potatoes, and leafy greens carry beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A. Eggs and dairy have some too.

Buddy nutrient

A little healthy fat. Vitamin A dissolves in fat, so a drizzle of olive oil or a handful of nuts helps your body take it in.

A gentle note

Orange and dark green vegetables are the friendly signposts. Eat the rainbow and you are most of the way there.

Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)

What it does

Vitamin B1 helps your body turn food into usable energy. It also supports healthy nerves.

Where to find it

Whole grains, brown rice, pork, beans, lentils, and sunflower seeds all carry it.

Buddy nutrient

Magnesium. Magnesium helps the enzymes that put B1 to work in your energy pathways.

A gentle note

Choosing whole grains over refined ones keeps more of this one on your plate.

Back to top

Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

What it does

Vitamin B2 helps your cells make energy from food. It also supports healthy eyes and skin.

Where to find it

Milk, yogurt, eggs, almonds, mushrooms, and leafy greens are good sources.

Buddy nutrient

Vitamin B6. Riboflavin helps your body switch B6 into its working form, so the two travel well together.

A gentle note

Dairy and eggs are easy daily carriers, and greens fill in the gaps.

Back to top

Vitamin B3 (Niacin)

What it does

Vitamin B3 helps your body release energy from food. It also supports healthy skin and digestion.

Where to find it

Chicken, turkey, tuna, peanuts, brown rice, and whole wheat all carry a good share.

Buddy nutrient

Tryptophan, an amino acid found in protein foods. Your body can make some niacin from tryptophan, which is why protein-rich meals lend a hand.

A gentle note

A varied diet with protein and whole grains keeps this one quietly covered.

Back to top

Vitamin B5 (Pantothenic Acid)

What it does

Vitamin B5 helps your body make energy and build some of the hormones it needs every day.

Where to find it

Almost everywhere: chicken, eggs, broccoli, mushrooms, avocados, and whole grains.

Buddy nutrient

The rest of the B-vitamin family. B5 does its energy work shoulder to shoulder with its B siblings.

A gentle note

Shortages are rare precisely because it hides in so many everyday foods.

Back to top

Vitamin B6

What it does

Vitamin B6 helps your brain make the chemical messengers it uses to talk to the body. It also supports immunity and helps your body use protein.

Where to find it

Chickpeas, chicken, fish, potatoes, and bananas are friendly sources.

Buddy nutrient

Magnesium. B6 helps your cells welcome magnesium in, and the two support calm nerves and steady energy together.

A gentle note

A banana or a scoop of chickpeas is a simple way to say hello to this one.

Back to top

Vitamin B9 (Folate)

What it does

Vitamin B9 helps your body make new cells, which matters for growth and everyday repair.

Where to find it

Leafy greens, beans, lentils, asparagus, and fortified grains carry it well.

Buddy nutrient

Vitamin B12. Folate and B12 work side by side in the cell-making process, each needing the other to finish the job.

A gentle note

Greens and beans are the classic pair on the plate and partners in the body too.

Back to top

Vitamin B12

What it does

Vitamin B12 keeps nerves healthy and helps make red blood cells. It also helps your body use energy from food.

Where to find it

Meat, fish, dairy, and eggs. Fortified cereals and plant milks carry it for those who skip animal foods.

Buddy nutrient

Folate (vitamin B9). The two work as a team in making healthy new cells.

A gentle note

This one comes almost entirely from animal foods or fortified ones, so it is worth knowing where yours comes from.

Back to top

Vitamin C

What it does

Vitamin C helps your body heal small cuts, keeps your gums healthy, and helps your immune system do its everyday work.

Where to find it

Oranges, strawberries, bell peppers, broccoli, and potatoes all carry a good share.

Buddy nutrient

Iron. Vitamin C helps your body take in iron from plant foods, so they make a good pair on the plate, like bell peppers with beans.

A gentle note

Your body does not store vitamin C for long, so a little from food each day is the kind way to keep topped up.

Back to top

Vitamin D

What it does

Vitamin D helps your body take in calcium, which keeps bones and teeth strong. It also supports everyday immune function.

Where to find it

Sunlight on your skin is the main source. Fatty fish like salmon, egg yolks, and fortified milk also carry it.

Buddy nutrient

Calcium. Vitamin D is the helper that lets your body actually use the calcium you eat, so the two travel together.

A gentle note

A little daily sunshine on your face and hands goes a long way, and food fills in the rest.

Back to top

Vitamin E

What it does

Vitamin E works as an antioxidant, protecting your cells from everyday wear. It also supports your immune system.

Where to find it

Almonds, sunflower seeds, peanut butter, spinach, and vegetable oils are all good sources.

Buddy nutrient

Vitamin C. Vitamin C helps refresh vitamin E after it has done its antioxidant work, so the two make a good team.

A gentle note

A small handful of nuts a day is a kind, easy way to keep this one covered.

Back to top

Vitamin K

What it does

Vitamin K helps your blood clot the way it should when you get a cut. It also helps keep bones strong.

Where to find it

Kale, spinach, collard greens, and broccoli are the stars. Smaller amounts show up in eggs and fermented foods.

Buddy nutrient

Calcium and vitamin D. Vitamin K helps guide calcium into your bones, where the bone-building crew puts it to work.

A gentle note

If you eat your greens regularly, you are giving this quiet worker everything it needs.

Minerals

Calcium

What it does

Calcium builds and keeps bones and teeth, and it helps muscles move and nerves carry their messages.

Where to find it

Milk, yogurt, and cheese are the familiar ones. Leafy greens like kale, almonds, and fortified plant milks carry it too.

Buddy nutrient

Vitamin D. Without its buddy, much of the calcium you eat passes through unused, which is why the pair matters.

A gentle note

Your bones are living tissue that rebuild a little every day, and steady calcium from food is how you feed the crew.

Back to top

Chloride

What it does

Chloride works with sodium to keep your body's fluids balanced. It is also part of the stomach acid that helps digestion.

Where to find it

Table salt is the main source. Celery, tomatoes, and olives carry small natural amounts too.

Buddy nutrient

Sodium. The two travel together in salt and in your body's fluid balance.

A gentle note

Like sodium, most people get plenty. A balanced diet with fruits and vegetables keeps the partnership in good shape.

Back to top

Chromium

What it does

Chromium helps your body use insulin to manage the sugar that comes from food.

Where to find it

Whole grains, broccoli, green beans, meat, and nuts.

Buddy nutrient

No single famous buddy. Chromium does its quiet work best as part of a varied diet with whole foods.

A gentle note

Choosing whole grains over refined ones is the simplest way to keep this one around.

Back to top

Copper

What it does

Copper helps your body make red blood cells, supports healthy nerves, and works with iron.

Where to find it

Shellfish, nuts, seeds, whole grains, mushrooms, and dark chocolate.

Buddy nutrient

Zinc. Copper and zinc balance each other, so eating a mix of whole foods keeps both on an even keel.

A gentle note

Nuts, seeds, and whole grains shared across the week keep this quiet helper steady.

Back to top

Iodine

What it does

Iodine helps your thyroid make the hormones that set the pace of your body's energy use.

Where to find it

Iodized salt, seafood, seaweed, dairy, and eggs.

Buddy nutrient

Selenium. Selenium also serves the thyroid, so the two look after that little gland together.

A gentle note

A little iodized salt in cooking is enough for most people. It is a small mineral with a big job.

Back to top

Iron

What it does

Iron helps your blood carry oxygen to every part of your body. Steady iron means steady energy.

Where to find it

Red meat and poultry are rich sources. Beans, lentils, spinach, and fortified cereals carry the plant kind.

Buddy nutrient

Vitamin C. Vitamin C helps your body take in iron from plant foods, so peppers with beans or orange with lentils is a smart plate.

A gentle note

Tea and coffee with meals can slow iron uptake a little, so give your iron-rich meal its own moment when you can.

Back to top

Magnesium

What it does

Magnesium helps muscles relax, supports calm nerves, and takes part in hundreds of small jobs inside your cells.

Where to find it

Almonds, pumpkin seeds, leafy greens, whole grains, beans, and dark chocolate.

Buddy nutrient

Vitamin D. Magnesium helps switch vitamin D into its active form, so the two support each other behind the scenes.

A gentle note

Leafy greens and a few squares of dark chocolate make this one a pleasant thing to keep up with.

Back to top

Manganese

What it does

Manganese helps your enzymes do their work, supports bone formation, and helps your body use carbohydrates and fats.

Where to find it

Whole grains, brown rice, nuts, leafy greens, and tea.

Buddy nutrient

No single famous buddy. Manganese does its quiet enzyme work best as part of a varied, balanced diet.

A gentle note

Whole grains and a cup of tea already give this one a comfortable home.

Back to top

Molybdenum

What it does

Molybdenum helps your enzymes break down certain substances found in food.

Where to find it

Beans, lentils, peas, whole grains, and nuts.

Buddy nutrient

No single famous buddy. It works quietly on its own and asks only for a varied diet around it.

A gentle note

If beans and whole grains show up in your week, this one is already taken care of.

Back to top

Phosphorus

What it does

Phosphorus teams up with calcium to build bones and teeth. It also helps your cells make energy.

Where to find it

Dairy, meat, fish, beans, nuts, and whole grains all carry it.

Buddy nutrient

Calcium. The two build bone together, which is why dairy and other calcium foods usually bring phosphorus along.

A gentle note

This one is easy to get from everyday foods, so it rarely needs a second thought.

Back to top

Potassium

What it does

Potassium helps balance fluids in your body, supports healthy blood pressure, and keeps muscles and nerves working smoothly.

Where to find it

Bananas, potatoes, sweet potatoes, beans, oranges, and spinach.

Buddy nutrient

Sodium. Potassium and sodium balance each other in your fluid systems, so the pair matters more than either one alone.

A gentle note

Fruits and vegetables are the natural home of potassium, another reason to keep them close.

Back to top

Selenium

What it does

Selenium works as an antioxidant and supports your thyroid and immune system.

Where to find it

Brazil nuts are famously rich, just one or two is plenty. Seafood, meat, and eggs carry it too.

Buddy nutrient

Iodine. Both serve the thyroid, so they make a natural pair.

A gentle note

A little goes a long way with this one. One Brazil nut now and then is more than enough.

Back to top

Sodium

What it does

Sodium helps your body balance fluids and keeps nerves and muscles firing properly.

Where to find it

Table salt is the main source, and it shows up naturally in small amounts in milk, meat, and vegetables like celery.

Buddy nutrient

Potassium. The two balance each other, so plenty of fruits and vegetables helps keep the partnership even.

A gentle note

Most people get plenty without trying. Flavoring with herbs and lemon lets you enjoy taste while keeping the balance.

Back to top

Zinc

What it does

Zinc supports your immune system, helps cuts heal, and keeps your senses of taste and smell sharp.

Where to find it

Meat, shellfish, chickpeas, lentils, nuts, seeds, and whole grains.

Buddy nutrient

Copper. Zinc and copper balance each other, like two friends on a seesaw, so a varied diet keeps both steady.

A gentle note

A mix of protein foods and whole grains through the week keeps this one in good shape.
